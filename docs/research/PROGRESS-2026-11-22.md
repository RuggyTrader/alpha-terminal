## 3. The central finding

**A hard 2 % drawdown measured from the running peak works like a stop on the whole account.**
* Once the account is ~2 % below its peak, no compliant policy can take new risk until a new peak is made. This is the "cash lock".
* Achievable growth is therefore bounded by roughly **2 % × (annual return ÷ worst single-period loss)**, and is lower in practice.
* Every directional spot stream we have (S3, A–D, buy-and-hold, H-L1) realizes **≈ 0.6–1.4 %/yr** at 2 %.
* Theoretical upper bounds are 2–6.5 %/yr, but cash lock and minimum orders prevent reaching them.

**What a strategy needs to be meaningful at 2 %:**
* a return-to-worst-day ratio **≳ 5–10**; and
* non-clustering losses.

None of ~2,930 configurations tested to date has that.

## 4. Unresolved, and what is worth doing next (ranked by expected usefulness)

1. **Yield on idle cash.**
   * Every 2 %-compliant strategy sits in cash >95 % of the time, so interest on that cash could exceed all trading profit.
   * Needs verification of which Canadian-available, low-risk options a $100 account can use, with custody risk stated.
   * This is a cost-and-return question, not a trading signal.
2. **5-minute intrabar resolver** (from the same official archive, `XBTUSD_5.csv` 68 MB).
   * Removes the fill-ordering ambiguity for any limit-order strategy.
   * Needed before any maker-based idea can be judged precisely.
3. **Decision for you, not for me:** whether the 2 % limit is meant as a hard trailing limit from the peak (as tested), or as, for example, a limit on loss of starting capital. The two give very different achievable returns. **The 2 % target was not changed.**
4. **Further hypotheses** must show a return-to-worst-day ratio ≥ 5 *before* full testing. The efficiency tool now makes that screen cheap. Candidates not yet tested:
   * intraday-rebalanced versions of S3 with resting stops, which reduce the worst-period loss;
   * diversification across strategies whose worst days do not coincide.
5. **Forward evidence** remains the only genuinely new evidence (Phase 4), after implementation of an approved strategy.
